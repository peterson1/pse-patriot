## 3. Trailing Offset computation

- Baseline (tight posture): `2.0 × ATR% = 2.0 × 4.2607% = 8.5213%`
- Adjustments applied: **+0.5 for liquidity deterioration only**
- Reason: mean daily turnover has fallen from ≈₱340k/day at the 10-Jun review to ≈₱263k/day
  (**−23%**), with a median of just ≈₱110k/day and sessions of 18,000 (08-Jul) and 19,000
  (15-Jul) shares. This is the same structural liquidity widening the prior review applied,
  at the same magnitude. `8.5213 + 0.5 = 9.0213`
- **Adjustments explicitly NOT applied, and why:**
  - **No gappiness premium.** The 17-Jul gap is already inside the ATR window (it contributes
    TR ₱0.11 of the ₱0.68 sum, 16% of the numerator). Adding a gap premium would double-count
    it.
  - **No spread premium.** The 4-tick/3.5% spread reading is a single intraday observation
    taken shortly after a 10% gap and is likely stressed. More decisively, it aims at a
    constraint the **baseline already satisfies**: at 8.5213% the stop sits ₱0.0971 = **9.7
    ticks** below the high-water mark, already ~2.4× the stressed 4-tick band and far outside
    the routine 1–2 tick band. A constraint met at zero cost cannot fund a widening.
- **Posture check (the guard against quiet widening):** expressed in ATR multiples —
  prior entry `11.0 ÷ 5.30 = 2.08×`; this entry `9.0 ÷ 4.26 = 2.11×`. Essentially flat, as a
  re-measurement should be. A 9.5% offset would have been `2.23×` — a ~7% **loosening** of the
  risk posture disguised as a tightening, and in peso terms a *wider* stop than last review
  (10.8 vs 10.2 ticks) despite volatility falling 20%. Rejected on that basis.
- Rounding: `9.0213% → ` **`9.0%`** (nearest 0.5). Within the 2–12.5% soft bounds.
- **Trailing Offset = 9.0%** (= ₱0.1026 = 10.3 ticks; stop would sit at ₱1.0374)

## 4. Limit Price (execution buffer) computation

- Baseline: `Trailing Offset ÷ 5 = 9.0 ÷ 5 = 1.80%`
- Adjustments applied: **widened to 2.75% — mandatory, not discretionary**
- Reason: the methodology's hard floor is `buffer ≥ 1 tick + typical spread`. At ₱1.14 one
  tick is ₱0.01 (**0.877%**); taking the conservative end of the prior review's routine 1–2
  tick spread gives a required minimum of **3 ticks = ₱0.03 = 2.6316%**. Walking the 0.25 grid:
  - `1.80%` = ₱0.0205 = 2.05 ticks → **fails** the floor (this is why the baseline is rejected)
  - `2.50%` = ₱0.0285 = 2.85 ticks → **fails** the floor
  - `2.75%` = ₱0.0314 = 3.14 ticks → **passes**
  2.75% is therefore the *smallest legal quarter-step*, not a generous one. It is also what
  2.6316 rounds to on a nearest-0.25 grid, so both routes converge.
- **Consistency note:** this buffer is derived from the **routine** 2-tick spread, so it stands
  even though §3 discards the stressed 4-tick reading. Fully covering that stressed spread
  would require 5 ticks = ₱0.05 = 4.39%, which over-fits a single observation and would pay
  ~1.6% of slippage on every routine fill. Not adopted.
- **Deliberate override of the ratio band (logged):** `2.75 ÷ 9.0 = 30.6%` of the offset,
  outside the stated 15–20% norm. This is unavoidable rather than discretionary — the ₱0.03
  tick floor alone forces ≥29% of offset at this price level. The methodology's peso floor is
  written as a MUST and governs the ratio guidance; illiquid micro-caps are explicitly allowed
  2–3%.
- PSE tick check: at ₱1.14 (₱0.50–4.99 band) one tick = ₱0.01. Buffer ≥ 1 tick + typical
  spread? **Yes** — ₱0.0314 vs ₱0.03 required. On the ₱0.01 order grid this places as a
  3-tick buffer, exactly 1 tick + a 2-tick spread.
- Rounding: already on a 0.25 step → **`2.75%`**
- **Limit Price = 2.75%**

Since the 10-Jun-2026 entry ASLAG rallied from ₱0.93 to ₱1.14 (**+22.6%**), with the bulk of
that arriving in a single unexplained 17-Jul session (+10.68% close-on-high on 1.293M shares,
5.25× the 20-day average). Realized volatility *fell* over the same period, so the offset
tightens; microstructure friction (tick size as a % of price, spread, thinner turnover)
*worsened*, so the buffer widens. **These two legs moving in opposite directions is expected,
not inconsistent** — the offset tracks realized volatility while the buffer tracks a peso-
denominated tick-and-spread floor that does not care about ATR. A system where the buffer
mechanically followed the offset would have cut it to 1.80% and produced a stop-limit at real
risk of never filling.

Note the near-identical peso stop distance across reviews (₱0.1023 then, ₱0.1026 now) despite
the 20% volatility decline — that is the higher price level, not posture drift; the ATR
multiple confirms the posture is flat.

## 9. Known limitations of this note

Recorded so the values are not read as more certain than the evidence supports.

1. **Single-vendor bar risk.** stockanalysis.com and ph.investing.com match to the centavo on
   all 20 overlapping sessions, but they may share an upstream feed (S&P Global). PSE EDGE
   confirmed only the 17-Jul close (₱1.14) and the 20-Jul intraday bar. Agreement between
   readers of one vendor retires *transcription* risk, not *vendor* risk. The prior 10-Jun
   review had a marginally stronger base here, so confidence has not increased between reviews.
2. **Disclosures could not be enumerated.** The EDGE disclosure feed is JS-rendered and
   returned empty to every attempt. Every "no news" finding in this research means *could not
   read the authoritative feed*, **not** *nothing was disclosed*. A rights offer, block sale,
   or contract award could exist unseen.
3. **The 17-Jul +10.68% move is unexplained.** On 5.25× normal volume, closing on the high, in
   an illiquid micro-cap — the signature of an event. The nearest dated item is a 13-Jul
   Trinasolar supplier PR about RASLAG-4, a plant operational since October 2024; that is a
   weak explanation and no causal link is established. The move has **not** been confirmed by a
   completed subsequent session (20-Jul was giving some back intraday, low ₱1.10). This bar is
   load-bearing twice over — it supplies TR ₱0.11 of the ₱0.68 sum *and* sets the ₱1.14
   denominator — so verify it before anchoring on these values.
4. **52-week range unverified.** ₱0.70/₱1.29 is published by three sources including EDGE, but
   the highest bar in ~51 retrieved sessions (07-May–17-Jul-2026) is ₱1.17 on 25-May. Attempts
   to extend the history returned the same default table. Plausible but unconfirmed; do not
   anchor on ₱1.29.
5. **Spread evidence is n=1.** The bid 1.10 / ask 1.14 reading is one intraday observation
   post-gap. The 1–2 tick routine spread that sets the limit floor comes from the prior review
   rather than fresh sampling. Sampling top-of-book at three fixed points per session over
   5–10 sessions would settle it and is the single highest-value follow-up for these values.
6. **2026 earnings figures were not verified** and are not used anywhere in this computation.
7. Vendor volumes are rounded to the nearest 1,000, so all turnover figures are approximate.
   Nothing was interpolated; no missing session was filled.
