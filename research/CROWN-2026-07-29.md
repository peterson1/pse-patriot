## 3. Trailing Offset computation

- Baseline (tight posture): `2.0 × ATR% = 2.0 × 1.12% = 2.24%`
- Adjustments applied: **widen +1.76pp for thin liquidity / measurement bias** (net result:
  unchanged at 4.0%)
- Reason for each adjustment: the measured ATR is **structurally understated** on this name. Four
  of the fourteen sessions printed TR = ₱0.00 and one (21-Jul) had no volume at all — the stock
  simply did not trade, which drags the average toward zero without meaning the risk is zero.
  Meanwhile the actual order book has thinned further since the 22-Jul note: the last five
  sessions traded ₱5k–₱59k of value per day, so a single seller can take out the bid by ₱0.01–0.02
  (0.6–1.1%) instantly. The thin-name premium therefore widens from +1.4pp to +1.76pp, which
  lands on the **same 4.0% offset** as the previous entry: the lower measured ATR and the worse
  liquidity cancel out. Holding the offset flat is the honest read, not a stale copy.
- Rounding: `2.24% → widen → ` **`4.0%`**
- **Trailing Offset = 4.0%**

## 4. Limit Price (execution buffer) computation

- Baseline: `Trailing Offset ÷ 5 = 4.0 ÷ 5 = 0.8%`
- Adjustments applied: **widen to 1.75%** for a materially thinner book than at the last review
- Reason for each adjustment: with sessions of 3,000 and 5,000 shares in the window, the touch
  can easily be several ticks wide when a stop actually fires. The prior 1.5% buffer (≈ 2.7 ticks)
  no longer comfortably clears "1 tick + typical spread" on a book this empty; 1.75% (≈ 3.1 ticks)
  restores the margin. This is the one value that should move: liquidity got worse, so the
  execution buffer widens even though the offset holds.
- PSE tick check: at ₱1.79 (₱0.50–₱4.99 band) one tick = **₱0.01 ≈ 0.559%**. Buffer 1.75% =
  ₱0.031 ≈ **3.1 ticks**, comfortably ≥ 1 tick + typical spread. ✔
- Rounding: `0.8% → widen → ` **`1.75%`**
- **Limit Price = 1.75%**
