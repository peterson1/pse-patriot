## 3. Trailing Offset computation

- Baseline (tight posture): `2.0 × ATR% = 2.0 × 1.12% = 2.23%`
- Adjustments applied: **widened for thin liquidity + gappiness** (structural, per methodology).
- Reason: Turnover is genuinely thin (~₱1.1M/day; far below liquid large-cap bank peers such as
  MBT/SECB/UBP which sit at 3.5%). The realized ATR% is artificially compressed by a string of
  near-flat, low-volume sessions, yet the name still produces gappy days (28-May printed a ₱1.35 /
  ~3.1% intraday range). A pure 2.23% stop on a thin name like this would get picked off by ordinary
  noise and could fill poorly. Holding the offset at **3.5%** keeps it directionally consistent with
  (slightly above) more-liquid bank peers, which is the correct posture for a thinner name — it
  should not sit tighter than the most liquid banks on the exchange.
- Rounding: `2.23% (raw) → widened to 3.5% → ` **`3.5%`** (already a clean half).
- **Trailing Offset = 3.5%**

## 4. Limit Price (execution buffer) computation

- Baseline: `Trailing Offset ÷ 5 = 3.5 ÷ 5 = 0.70%`
- Adjustments applied: **slight widen for thin liquidity / wider spreads** (0.70% → 0.75%).
- Reason: On a thin name the buffer must comfortably exceed the bid–ask spread so the stop-limit
  still fills in a fast drop. Rounding to 0.75% gives a touch more fill insurance at negligible cost.
- PSE tick check: at ₱43.50 the price band is ₱20.00–₱49.95, so one tick = **₱0.05** (≈0.115%).
  The 0.75% buffer = **₱0.33 ≈ 6–7 ticks**, comfortably ≥ one tick + a typical few-tick spread. ✓
- Rounding: `0.70% (raw) → 0.75%` **`0.75%`**
- **Limit Price = 0.75%**
