## 3. Trailing Offset computation

- Baseline (tight posture): `2.0 × ATR% = 2.0 × 2.42% = 4.83%`
- Adjustments applied: widen for **thin liquidity** and **fresh breakout/event volatility**.
- Reason for each adjustment:
  - *Liquidity:* ~₱1.5M/day value turnover is genuinely thin. A stop placed at the bare 2×
    baseline on a low-turnover name gets picked off by noise and fills poorly — methodology
    says nudge the offset up for low turnover.
  - *Gappiness / event risk:* today's +7.5% momentum breakout to ₱11.00 (after weeks of
    orderly ₱0.15–0.35 daily ranges) signals a more volatile, news-driven phase — a touch
    more room is warranted so the trade isn't whipsawed out on a pullback within the breakout.
- Rounding: `4.83% →` nudged up for the two structural reasons above → **`5.5%`** (nearest 0.5).
- **Trailing Offset = 5.5%**

## 4. Limit Price (execution buffer) computation

- Baseline: `Trailing Offset ÷ 5 = 5.5 ÷ 5 = 1.1%`
- Adjustments applied: widen modestly for thin liquidity / wider spread.
- Reason for each adjustment: ~₱1.5M/day turnover means a wider bid–ask; in a fast drop the
  stop-limit needs enough buffer to actually clear the spread and fill. Nudge 1.1% → 1.25%.
- PSE tick check: at ₱10.20 the PSE tick is **₱0.02** (₱10.00–₱19.98 band). A 1.25% buffer =
  ₱0.128 ≈ **6 ticks**, comfortably ≥ one tick + a typical 1–2-tick spread on a thin name.
  (Note DD sits just above the ₱10.00 band edge where the tick steps up from ₱0.01 to ₱0.02.)
- Rounding: `1.1% →` **`1.25%`** (nearest 0.25, widened side for thin liquidity).
- **Limit Price = 1.25%**
