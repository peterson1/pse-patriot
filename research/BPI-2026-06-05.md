## 3. Trailing Offset computation

- Baseline (tight posture): `2.0 × ATR% = 2.0 × 2.576% = 5.15%`
- Adjustments applied: none (no widening for liquidity — BPI is a deep, high-turnover blue chip with tight spreads).
- Reason: although there were two recent up-gaps (May 14, May 29), they were upside spikes (favorable for a long's trailing stop, which only fires on downside) and the day-to-day downside ranges have been orderly; no structural reason to widen above baseline.
- Rounding: `5.15% →` **`5.0%`** (nearer to 5.0 than 5.5; the high liquidity supports keeping the tighter half).
- **Trailing Offset = 5.0%**

## 4. Limit Price (execution buffer) computation

- Baseline: `Trailing Offset ÷ 5 = 5.0 ÷ 5 = 1.0%`
- Adjustments applied: tightened toward the blue-chip range, then a small widen above the prior 0.25 for recent gappiness.
- Reason: BPI is one of the most liquid PSE banks (deep order book, tight 1–2 tick spread), so the methodology steers blue chips toward 0.25–0.5% rather than the formula's 1.0%. I did **not** keep the very-tight 0.25% used previously: the May 14 (+7.7%) and May 29 (+3.9%) gaps show the name can move fast, so a slightly larger buffer (0.50%) gives the stop-limit better odds of filling if it triggers during one of those fast moves, while still sitting inside the blue-chip band.
- PSE tick check: at ₱95.10 the PSE tick (band ₱50.00–₱99.99) is ₱0.05 (≈0.053%). A 0.50% buffer = ₱0.48 ≈ 9.5 ticks; a 0.25% buffer = ₱0.24 ≈ 4.8 ticks. Either comfortably exceeds one tick + a typical 1–2 tick spread. **Buffer ≥ 1 tick + spread? Yes.**
- Rounding: `1.0% → 0.50%` (logged widen of the blue-chip tighten; already on a 0.25 grid) → **`0.50%`**
- **Limit Price = 0.50%**
